## "Anything else you'd like us to know?"

> Two months ago, in *The Lancet*, PCOS was officially renamed **PMOS — Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome** — after an 11-year global consensus, specifically to stop framing it as a cyst-and-fertility problem and recognise it as a lifelong metabolic-endocrine condition affecting 1 in 8 women. Every product in the market still treats it the old way. We're building for the new definition.
>
> Indian women with PMOS wait around seven months for a diagnosis, get told "come back when you want a baby," and use period apps that break on the irregular cycles that *define* the condition. What they actually ask for, in their own words, is *"help me find what personally influences my wellbeing."* Nobody builds that — because PMOS is an experiment of one, and no app personalises it.
>
> So our core is an **n-of-1 personalisation engine**: it treats an irregular cycle as signal rather than error, discovers each woman's own cause→effect patterns across sleep, movement, mood, skin and supplements, runs structured 8–12 week experiments that end in an honest verdict (including "no detectable change"), and coaches evidence-graded daily levers where every claim carries its certainty grade. It escalates properly too — a cycle-interval sentinel for endometrial risk, validated mental-health screening (anxiety is 6× more common in this group), and a doctor-ready dossier that attacks the diagnostic delay. Weight-neutral by guideline, dignity-first by design, and zero supplement commerce by principle: we can be honest about the evidence precisely because we never sell the pills.
>
> This isn't a weekend project for us. I run **mycyclesafe.ai**, a privacy-first AI cycle tracker with 500,000+ users across 120 countries — and the users whose cycles break our own predictions *are*, by definition, this audience. We already have the distribution, the trust, and (with fresh consent) the cycle history to launch this properly. The hackathon is where we build the core engine.
>
> Not a diagnostic — a well-being companion that knows when to hand you to a doctor.
