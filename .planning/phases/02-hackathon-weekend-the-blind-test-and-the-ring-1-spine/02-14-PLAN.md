<objective>
Close the weekend: assemble the gate ledger, apply the cut list in its fixed order, freeze the build,
rehearse three times, and confirm the fallback demo is playable with no app and no phone.

Purpose: HACK-10 requires the five gates to have been enforced with named owners and each decided
within ten minutes, and the cuts called in a fixed order by Dhodraj. HACK-11 requires the fallback
demo to always be available. Neither is provable at the end unless it was recorded along the way —
this plan is where that record is assembled, checked for gaps, and turned into the thing the team
presents.

Build-plan.md §9 is explicit about who enforces the last gate: "Rahul enforces this one, not Dhodraj,
because by then Dhodraj will want to keep going and should not." That is not a formality. Judgement
degrades before typing does, and the person calling the freeze should be the one who has not spent
twenty-eight hours inside the product.

The last item of the demo is what we cut and why — build-plan.md §13 calls it the strongest thing
about the team. It is only strong if the cut log is honest.

Output: a frozen build, a complete gate ledger, an honest cut log, and three rehearsals.

Owners:
- **Rahul Ramakrishnan** — Task 2, the freeze. He calls it and nobody overrides him.
- **Dhodraj Sundaram** — Task 1's cut calls. Build-plan.md §10: "Dhodraj calls each cut. Not a vote."
- **Satheeshkumar** — Task 3. He owns the laptop, the cable, the adapter, the spare device and the
  recording (build-plan.md §13), and he runs the demo.
- **Belson Johnson** — applies the cut switches in his own files when Dhodraj calls them.
</objective>
