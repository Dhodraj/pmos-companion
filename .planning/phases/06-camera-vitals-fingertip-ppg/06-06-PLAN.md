<objective>
Complete the signal chain: detect beats in the time domain, cross-check them against the frequency
domain, turn agreement and signal quality into a confidence level and a range, gate out captures
that fail saturation, motion or AC/DC checks with a specific reason each, and assemble the whole
thing into one entry point that returns the frozen result union within the 3-second budget.

Purpose: this is where the feature either stays honest or starts lying. A single detection method
producing a plausible wrong number is worse than a rejection, because a rejection tells her to try
again and a wrong number gets fed into her correlation engine and treated as evidence about her own
body. Everything here is built so the pipeline would rather say no.

Output: five tested modules and a single `analyzeCapture` entry point.
</objective>

  <behavior>
    - On the first clean fixture, peak detection recovers the fixture's declared rate within 2 beats per minute.
    - On the second clean fixture at a different declared rate, it does the same — an estimator accidentally tuned to one frequency fails this.
    - The FFT cross-check independently recovers each clean fixture's declared rate within 2 beats per minute, mapping the dominant bin to a rate correctly for the actual sample rate and zero-padded length.
    - On the pure-noise null control, the two estimators disagree beyond threshold and the result is a rejection, not a rate. A confident answer on noise is a failing test.
    - When the two estimates agree closely, confidence is high; moderate disagreement inside threshold yields medium or low; disagreement beyond threshold rejects.
    - The produced range is never narrower than plus or minus 5 beats per minute, at any confidence level, on a single capture.
    - No exported function returns a bare scalar rate; every public return is the frozen result union or a structure carrying both bounds.
  </behavior>
  <action>
Install `fft.js` pinned to its 4.x line, only after the preceding checkpoint is approved. Do not
hand-write a DFT: windowing, zero-padding and bin-to-frequency mapping are each easy to get subtly
wrong in ways that produce a plausible number rather than an error.

Implement peak detection over the band-limited signal from Plan 06-03: find beats, convert
inter-beat intervals to a rate, and reject physiologically impossible intervals rather than letting
one missed or doubled beat drag the mean. Report the dispersion of the intervals alongside the
estimate — that dispersion is one of the inputs to confidence.

Implement the FFT cross-check with `fft.js`: window the signal, zero-pad to a power of two, take the
magnitude spectrum, find the dominant bin inside the 0.7 to 3.5 Hz band, and map it back to a rate
using the actual sample rate. Interpolate around the dominant bin rather than taking its centre
frequency raw, because at 30 samples per second over 30 seconds the bin spacing is coarse relative
to the precision being claimed.

Then combine them, and this is the part that matters. If the two methods disagree by more than the
threshold, do not average them — return a rejection. Averaging two disagreeing estimators is exactly
how a pipeline produces a confident wrong number, and this project's own threat register names a
single faulty detection method silently producing a wrong rate as the integrity risk of this
feature. Set the threshold explicitly as a named constant with a comment recording where the value
came from.

Size the range from single-capture error expectations, not from the published lab figure. The
1.6 percent mean absolute percentage error in the validation study is an aggregate across many
readings under controlled conditions; this project's own feature spec already plans for 5 to 8 beats
per minute of single-measurement error. Enforce a minimum half-width of 5 beats per minute at every
confidence level, and widen it as agreement and interval dispersion worsen. A narrow range is a
false-precision claim, and this feature is required not to make one.

Write every test above first, watch it fail, then implement.
