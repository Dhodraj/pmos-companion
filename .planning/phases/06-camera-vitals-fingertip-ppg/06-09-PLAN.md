<objective>
Close the phase on its release gate: read every user-facing string against the anxiety this
population carries, get the clinician's answer recorded and incorporated, and then make an explicit,
human, one-way decision about whether this feature reaches users.

Purpose: this feature ships at P0 over a practising clinician's recorded objection that a resting
heart rate would not change a woman's care. The team decided to build it anyway and to gate it on
answering that objection in writing. This plan is where that promise is kept or the feature stays
off. Turning the flag on is not reversible in the way code is — a woman who was handed a cardiac
number by a health app that had not answered its own clinical objection cannot be un-handed it, and
the trust cost lands on a product whose entire proposition is being honest about what it does and
does not know.

Output: a written anxiety-safety review, a reviewed clinical position document, and a recorded
release decision.
</objective>

at the size and in the context she will. For each one, write down an answer to three questions in

attribute the failure to her or to the situation; and would a woman who has been dismissed by
clinicians for years read it as one more thing she is doing wrong.

Review the failure strings hardest. Most captures that fail will fail for physical reasons she has no
control over — cold hands, poor peripheral perfusion, a camera that cannot hold its exposure — and a
string that reads as an instruction she failed to follow is the wrong string even when it is
technically accurate. Failure is the normal case here by design; the copy has to sound like it.

Review the result strings for false reassurance in the other direction too. A range presented too
warmly can imply an all-clear this feature is not entitled to give. The honest register is neutral:
here is what was measured, here is how sure we are, this is a wellness trend and not a clinical
measurement.

Review the waveform as a visual, not only the words around it. The original clinician brief asked
whether a visible pulse trace reassures or alarms this population, and nobody answered. Look at it
running on a phone and record your own judgement: does it read as a calm animation or as a hospital
monitor. That judgement goes into this document and into the material the reviewer sees in Task 2.

proposed replacement. Then apply the accepted replacements to the copy modules directly, in this
task, so the review is a change and not a memo. Where you propose a change that alters meaning
rather than tone, flag it for Belson rather than applying it unilaterally.

Record explicitly which findings, if any, you consider blocking for release. Those go to the
