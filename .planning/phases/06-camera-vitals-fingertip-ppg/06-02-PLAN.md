<objective>
Start the one deliverable in this phase that has an external dependency and an unknown turnaround:
the written answer to a practising clinician's recorded objection that a resting heart rate "is not
a marker for this condition, not in the guideline, and would not change her care". Draft it, send it
to a named reviewer, and make the release gate mechanical so it cannot be forgotten under deadline
pressure.

Purpose: OPEN-01 has stood since ingest and blocks Phase 6 release. It has an external turnaround
the engineering work does not — a clinician answers on their timetable, not the team's — so it
starts in Wave 1, in parallel with the tracer, and closes in Plan 06-09. It is also the honest part:
this feature ships at P0 over an unresolved, unrebutted clinical objection, and the only defensible
way to do that is to answer the objection in writing before a user sees the feature.

Output: a real document with real citations, a live review request with a date on it, and a CI check
that fails the build if the flag is on and the document is not reviewed.
</objective>

**The objection, verbatim.** Quote the recorded objection exactly as it appears in
`hackathon/PMOS-clinical-review.pdf`, attributed and dated. Do not paraphrase it into something
easier to answer.

**What we concede.** State plainly, in the document's own voice, that resting heart rate is not a
diagnostic or management marker for PMOS; that the 2023 International Evidence-Based PCOS Guideline's
recommended cardiovascular assessments are BMI, glucose screening, annual blood pressure and lipid
screening, and do not include heart rate or heart-rate variability; and that on today's standard of
care the clinician's answer was correct. Flag honestly that this last point rests on secondary
guideline coverage because the primary source returned an HTTP 403 to the research pass — ask the
reviewing clinician to confirm or correct it from their own access.

**What the literature actually supports.** Engage with the two papers by name, with their own
caveats intact:

- Yu, Chen, Zheng et al., "The role of the autonomic nervous system in polycystic ovary syndrome",
  *Frontiers in Endocrinology*, 19 Jan 2024, PMC10834786 — replicated increased sympathetic and
  decreased parasympathetic activity, including directly-measured muscle sympathetic nerve activity,
  independent of BMI; and the paper's own statement that larger long-term studies are needed before
  clinical application, describing autonomic testing as having clinical application prospect rather
  than current use.
- Islam, Motdhare, Bejoy, Wilkhoo, "Autonomic dysfunction and resting heart rate abnormalities as
  early cardiovascular signals in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS): a focused review",
  *Exploration of Cardiology*, 12 Feb 2026 — 32 studies synthesised, elevated resting heart rate and
  impaired HRV even in normal-weight or metabolically mild phenotypes; and the paper's own statement
  that resting heart rate should not be used in isolation, is non-specific, and is supportive rather
  than definitive. Note that this journal's indexing status was not independently verified, so the
  reviewer can weight it themselves.

Read both papers directly before writing this section. Do not restate the research summary — it is a
WebFetch summary, and a rebuttal built on a summary of a summary will not survive review.

**The reframe, which is the actual argument.** The claim this document makes is not that the
clinician was wrong. It is that the feature was mis-described to them. Fingertip heart rate is not
offered as a clinical marker with population-level meaning; it is one more input variable into the
same per-user correlation engine that already ingests sleep, skin, mood, food and supplements
(Phase 4), held to exactly the same confidence floor and the same still-learning honesty standard as
every other logged signal. Its legitimacy as a per-user correlate does not depend on resting heart
rate being an established population-level marker — it depends only on the engine's existing refusal
to report a pattern it cannot support. The emerging autonomic-dysfunction literature is cited as the
reason this specific population is more scientifically interesting to watch than a generic wellness
user, and explicitly not as evidence the number is actionable on its own.

**The safety question nobody answered.** The original brief asked four questions about camera heart
rate in a population with roughly 6x baseline anxiety: whether presenting an objective cardiac
number to this group is wise; what guidance should accompany a reading; what should happen on an
abnormal or implausible reading; and whether a visible pulse waveform is reassuring or alarming. The
recorded review answered only the "what would it tell her" objection. Put all four to the reviewer
explicitly, and state what the build currently does about each: the reading is shown as a range with
a confidence and never as a single precise digit (PPG-04); it is labelled a wellness trend and never
a clinical measurement (DOC-04, REG-02); failure is presented as the normal case with specific
guidance and breaks no streak (PPG-05); and the waveform renders live during capture (PPG-03) —
which is the specific thing the reviewer is being asked to judge.

**What we are asking for.** Three numbered questions, each answerable in a sentence: does the
reframing hold; is the anxiety-safety posture adequate or does something need to change before
release; and is there anything in the build as described that crosses the screen-and-refer line.

**Front matter.** Give the file YAML front matter with `status: draft`, `reviewed: false`,
`reviewer: null`, `reviewer_qualification: null`, `review_date: null`, `sent_date: null`. Plan 06-09
flips these; the CI gate in Task 3 reads them.

Also write `docs/clinical/README.md` explaining what lives in this directory and that the position
document gates the camera-vitals release.
