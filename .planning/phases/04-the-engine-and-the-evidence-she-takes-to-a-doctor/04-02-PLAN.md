<objective>
Take the tracer's single-pair engine to product grade: real per-cycle-phase baselines with a
shrinkage rule for the irregular cycles this product exists to serve, the full pairwise sweep across
her curated symptom dimensions, Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied across one declared family,
a confidence gate that reads the corrected p-value, a weekly reveal cadence, and a suppression-rate

actually use — roughly 105 variable pairs, each searched across 14 lags — which is precisely where
multiple testing stops being an academic concern and starts inventing patterns out of noise.

methodology document the "how do you know that?" panel will render in Plan 04-04.

  <name>Task 1 [Dhodraj Sundaram + Satheeshkumar]: Declare the multiple-testing correction family</name>
  <decision>Across which family of hypotheses does Benjamini-Hochberg correction apply — one test per variable pair at that pair's best lag, or one test per pair per lag across the whole grid?</decision>
  <context>
With roughly 15 curated symptom dimensions there are 105 pairs. Searching each across 14 lags gives
either 105 simultaneous hypotheses (one summary per pair, at its strongest lag) or about 1,470 (the
full grid). Benjamini-Hochberg's threshold scales with family size, so the two conventions produce
materially different suppression behaviour on identical data: the full grid is roughly fourteen
times stricter and will show "still learning" far more often.

This is a one-way door. `INSIGHT.surfaced` is a stored field in the data model; every finding
already computed and shown to a user carries the semantics of whichever convention produced it.
Changing the family later does not just alter future behaviour — it silently redefines what every
existing stored insight meant, and re-deriving them requires re-running the engine over history
under the new family and reconciling anything already revealed or exported to a doctor.

The engine's research recommends per-pair-at-best-lag: it matches the product's own single-lag
framing ("your skin flares two to three days after nights under six hours"), it matches the
hand-rolled approach Phase 2 already took, and selecting the best lag is a summarisation step
rather than 14 independent claims. Both conventions are statistically defensible. Note that
best-lag selection is itself a form of multiple comparison, so the per-pair option is the less
conservative of the two and should be paired with the suppression-rate monitor in Task 3.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="per-pair-best-lag">
      <name>One test per variable pair, at that pair's strongest lag (recommended)</name>
      <pros>Family size 105. Matches the single-lag story the product tells her. Matches Phase 2's engine. Reveals appear early enough that the retention ladder's "week 6+ first weak-signal observations" is reachable.</pros>
      <cons>Best-lag selection is an unaccounted comparison, so the effective false-discovery rate is somewhat higher than the nominal q. Requires the suppression monitor to catch drift.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="full-grid">
      <name>One test per pair per lag, across the full 1,470-hypothesis grid</name>
      <pros>Statistically the most conservative and the most defensible to a clinician reviewer. No unaccounted selection step.</pros>
      <cons>Roughly fourteen times stricter. "Still learning" becomes the dominant state for months, which risks reading as a broken product rather than an honest one, and undercuts NOF1-05's one-reveal-per-week goal.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="per-pair-with-selection-penalty">
      <name>Per pair at best lag, with an explicit penalty for the lag search</name>
      <pros>Keeps the product's single-lag framing while pricing in the selection step.</pros>
      <cons>No settled convention for the penalty in an n-of-1 context; introduces a tuning constant with no external reference, which is harder to defend than either clean option.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select: per-pair-best-lag, full-grid, or per-pair-with-selection-penalty</resume-signal>
</task>
