<objective>
Make the reveal answerable. She can ask "how do you know that?" and get the method in plain words
with her own numbers in it — because the finding came from a lagged cross-correlation with a
bootstrap interval and an FDR correction, and that chain is written down. And she gets a sentence
that reads like a person wrote it, phrased by Gemini from the Cloudflare Worker, on abstracted
numbers only, checked against the numbers the engine computed before it is shown, falling back to
the deterministic sentence the moment anything is off.

Purpose: this closes the third of the phase's four success criteria and puts a guarded boundary
around the only place in this phase where anything leaves the device. It also resolves an open gap
the research surfaced and left open: PRIV-01's six consent classes name nothing that covers sending
an abstracted numeric finding to a third-party model for phrasing. This plan resolves it rather than
leaving it — a seventh consent class, in the data plane, visible and revocable.

Scope boundary, decided deliberately and stated here rather than left implicit: **the model-phrased
doctor-summary variant is descoped from this phase.** The doctor one-pager's opening paragraph is
Plan 04-03's deterministic `renderDoctorNarrative`, always, for every user, in every consent state.
DOC-02 is satisfied by that deterministic narrative — it is already written, already human-reviewed
in Plan 04-05, and already inside the sub-ten-second budget Plan 04-03 measured on the demo phone.
See "Descoped from this phase" below for the full reasoning and for what adding the phrased variant
back would require. Everything this plan builds serves exactly one surface: the insight-finding
sentence and the explanation behind it.

Output: the seventh consent class and its first-use disclosure, the Worker phrasing route with its
prompt and output guard, the consent-gated client seam for insight findings, and the "how do you
know that?" panel.
</objective>

  <name>Task 1 [Dhodraj Sundaram + Satheeshkumar]: How is AI phrasing of derived patterns consented?</name>
  <decision>Does sending an abstracted numeric finding to Gemini for phrasing get its own consent class, fold into the existing cycle-and-symptom class, or require an explicit opt-in before the first reveal is phrased?</decision>
  <context>
PRIV-01 requires purpose-bound granular consent per data class and names exactly six: cycle and
symptom data, camera sensing, skin images, mental-health screeners, mycyclesafe import, microphone.
None of them names "send an abstracted numeric finding to a third-party language model for
phrasing." The research found this gap and left it open. It has to be closed before a phrasing call
is wired into the reveal path, not after.

What actually crosses the network is small and abstracted: two symptom dimension names drawn from
the fixed curated set, a lag in days, a direction, an effect-size band, an observation count and a
confidence interval. No dates, no raw severities, no free text, no identifiers. It is her pattern,
not her record. But DPDP's purpose limitation is about the purpose, not the payload size, and
"phrased by a third-party model" is a materially different purpose from "stored on your phone so
the app can show you a cycle view."

This is a one-way door. Consent rows are written per user at onboarding and each row carries the
purpose it was granted for. Adding a class later means a schema migration over rows already written,
and defaulting a newly-added class to granted for users who never saw it is precisely the DPDP
failure Phase 3's own research flagged. The choice made here is the one every existing user's
consent ledger inherits.

Recommendation, and what this plan implements unless redirected: a seventh class, `ai_phrasing`,
defaulting to granted because what crosses is derived numbers rather than health records — paired
with a first-use disclosure sheet shown before the first phrasing request ever fires, naming
exactly what leaves the phone and carrying an immediate off switch. The disclosure is what makes
"default granted" defensible rather than silent: she is told at the moment of first use, not buried
in an onboarding flow she tapped through weeks earlier. Revoking it stops the request at the client
and the deterministic sentence is used, which is a complete product either way.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="seventh-class-default-on-with-disclosure">
      <name>Seventh class `ai_phrasing`, default granted, with a first-use disclosure and an immediate off switch (recommended)</name>
      <pros>Purpose-bound and independently revocable, which is what PRIV-01 actually asks for. The disclosure converts a default into an informed one. Reveals read naturally from day one. Revoking degrades to the deterministic sentence with nothing lost structurally.</pros>
      <cons>A newly-added class defaulting to granted is the pattern Phase 3's research warned about; the disclosure is what carries the defence, so the disclosure is not optional. Requires a Dexie schema version bump with an upgrade that seeds the row for existing users.</cons>

## Descoped from this phase

**What is descoped:** the model-phrased doctor-summary paragraph. Earlier drafts of this plan routed
the one-pager's opening paragraph through the phrasing seam when consent was granted, falling back
to Plan 04-03's `renderDoctorNarrative` otherwise. That variant is removed. The deterministic
narrative is the paragraph, in every case.

**Why, in four parts:**

1. *It was internally contradictory as specified.* Task 3's route accepts one shape — two symptom
   dimension names, lag days, direction, effect-size band, observation count, confidence-interval
   bounds — and is acceptance-tested to reject any body carrying a field outside it. An export
   aggregate is nothing but fields outside it. The doctor-summary request would have been rejected
   by the very route it depended on, and because the design falls back on every failure, it would
   have failed silently and permanently while looking like it worked.
2. *No test in the plan could have caught that.* "Renders correctly with consent granted" cannot
   distinguish a genuinely phrased paragraph from one that fell back on the first call and every
   call after. Making it distinguishable means a second schema, a second prompt, a second
   numeric-echo guard with its own echo targets, and acceptance criteria that can tell the two
   sentences apart — a second full guarded surface for a paragraph that already reads well.
3. *It put a network round trip inside a measured budget.* Plan 04-03 established and
   human-verified a ten-second tap-to-document budget on the demo phone, with the radio off, over a
   purely local path. Adding a live third-party call into that path — defaulted ON by Task 1's
   recommended consent decision, so the common case, not the edge case — makes the budget a budget
   over a network. Honouring it would have meant a reconciled timeout, an extended `pdf-perf.test.ts`
   and a re-measured consent-granted path. Real work, in a phase already carrying a hand-rolled
   FDR-corrected engine and that same ten-second budget.
4. *DOC-02 is already satisfied without it.* The requirement is a compiled export a clinician can
   read in seven minutes. The deterministic narrative delivers that, is assembled from the payload's
   own numbers, is asserted non-causal by a banned-phrase test in Plan 04-03, and is read by
   Satheeshkumar against a written rubric in Plan 04-05. The phrased variant was an improvement in
   register, not a requirement — a nice-to-have bought with a second guarded model surface.

**What this costs.** `hackathon/proposal.md` says Gemini "writes the doctor-facing summary from
structured data", and RESEARCH.md's Architectural Responsibility Map carries a row for it. That
claim is not delivered in this phase. It is deferred, not deleted, and the deferral is recorded here
and annotated in RESEARCH.md so it cannot be lost. Gemini's language role in this phase is still
real and still demonstrable: it phrases the insight finding she reads on the reveal.

**What adding it back would require** — for whoever picks this up in a later phase, so they start
from the reasoning rather than from a stub:

- A discriminated request shape on the phrasing route (`kind: 'finding' | 'doctor-summary'`) with a
  second declared schema for the aggregate fields, its own prompt file, and its own numeric-echo
  guard checking the interval statistics and the record-window length rather than the observation
  count and lag.
- Acceptance criteria that distinguish a phrased paragraph from a fallback — at minimum a test
  asserting the phrased path was exercised and its output differs from `renderDoctorNarrative`,
  so a permanently-falling-back implementation fails rather than passes.
- A timeout reconciled against the remaining share of the ten-second budget, with the export's
  fallback firing well inside it.
- `src/export/__tests__/pdf-perf.test.ts` extended to cover the consent-granted branch, and a human
  re-measurement on the demo phone in that state — not only the consent-revoked one, which is the
  path that never touches the network and therefore proves nothing about timing.
